## Hosting

The site runs on Cloudflare. To deliver and protect it, Cloudflare processes technical data for every request: IP address, browser, requested page and time.

- **Why:** to deliver the site and keep it secure, including blocking attack traffic.
- **Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is a site that loads and is protected against misuse.
- **Who receives it:** Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. Cloudflare processes this data on my behalf under a data processing agreement. For some data, such as data used to secure its own network, Cloudflare acts on its own responsibility. See [Cloudflare's privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).
- **Outside the EU:** Cloudflare may process the data in the USA and other countries. Cloudflare is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, which the European Commission has found adequate (Art. 45 GDPR). Its [data processing agreement](https://www.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-customer-dpa/) also includes the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46 GDPR).
- **Error logs:** Cloudflare keeps request logs for this site in my account. I use them only to find and fix errors. They are deleted after 3 days.
- **How long:** apart from those logs, I keep no logs of my own. Cloudflare shows me aggregated traffic and security statistics for about a month. Cloudflare deletes its own records once it no longer needs them for delivery and security; it doesn't publish fixed periods.

## Your rights

You can ask for access to your data (Art. 15 GDPR), correction (Art. 16), deletion (Art. 17) and restriction of processing (Art. 18). Write to privacy [at] visua1hue.dev. I usually can't link request data to a person, so I may ask for details that help me find yours (Art. 11 GDPR).

## Right to object

You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your situation, to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (Art. 21 GDPR). I will then stop unless I can show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, or the processing is needed for legal claims.

## Right to complain

You can complain to a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), in particular in the EU country where you live or work or where you think the violation happened.
